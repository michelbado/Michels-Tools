Subscribers: the private addresses behind the NAT

Networks (100.64.0.0/22), ranges (100.64.0.1-100.64.3.254) or single addresses, one per line or separated by commas. Subscribers are numbered in the order you type them.

Public pool: the addresses they share

Every address of the pool is used, including the first and last of a network.

Ports

Ports below 1024 are normally kept for the NAT itself. This is the plain layout: subscriber 1 gets the first block of the first public address, subscriber 2 the next block, and so on. Your equipment may lay blocks out in its own order, so check the result against its own deterministic-NAT tool or log before you rely on it.

    Who is behind a public address and port? (the question a complaint or a legal request asks)

    The answer is only right for the date the layout was in force: write down the layout, and the date it started and ended, together with the time of the event you are tracing. A different layout on that day gives a different subscriber.
